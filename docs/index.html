Boring Tracker

An iPhone app for writing down numbers.

For tracking whatever you want — macros, habits, or any other data. Free, open source, no ads, no accounts, no subscription, no server, no feature bloat. Type a number, done.

Most other macro trackers want you to scan a barcode for every ingredient of a dish you cooked yourself. It takes a lot of time for no benefit. This one just lets you write down 600 calories, 40 protein and done, as fast as possible, minimum clicks, minimum wait/lag.

You can also use it to track anything you want: your cat's weight, pushups, blood glucose, the last time you changed a filter. It keeps the history, adds up your day, and draws a plain graph.

Everything stays on your own device, and you can export all of it as json/csv or import it back. My goal with it is to behave like a built-in iOS app (e.g. the calculator). It is boring, but does its job, hence the name.

What it refuses to do

These are rules, not preferences. They are written down in the repository as PHILOSOPHY.md, so anyone can hold the app to them.

  • No ads. Ever, in any form, including recommended products and affiliate links.
  • No subscription. Free, all of it, with no pro tier, no paid unlock and no tip jar that nags.
  • No account. Nothing to sign up for and nothing to log in to.
  • No server. There is no backend, so there is nothing to leak, nothing to pay for, and nothing that can switch the app off in 2029.
  • No telemetry. No analytics, no crash reporting SDK, no anonymous usage data.
  • No third-party code. Apple's frameworks and nothing else — third-party SDKs are how ads, telemetry and rot get in.
  • No lock-in. Your data exports in one tap, to a plain readable file, and imports back.

Privacy policy

Last updated 19 August 2026.

Boring Tracker collects no data. Not anonymised data, not aggregated data, not diagnostics. There is no developer copy of anything you type into it.

The app is open source under the MIT licence, so every sentence below can be checked against the code that this page is served from rather than taken on trust. Where a claim names a file, that file is in the same repository.

No servers, and no network

There is no backend service behind this app and no account system. The app contains no networking code at all — no request is made to the developer or to anyone else, so nothing you enter can leave the device by itself.

The one link out is the Source on GitHub row on the About screen. If you tap it, your browser opens GitHub in the ordinary way; that is your browser talking to GitHub, and it carries nothing about your data with it.

What is stored, and where

Everything the app knows lives on your device, inside the app's own container, in two places:

  • Your trackers and every entry, as a single JSON file (Application Support/boring-tracker/store.json), with a backup copy kept beside it so a bad write cannot cost you your history.
  • Three interface preferences: light or dark appearance, what hour your day starts at, and which group of trackers you used last.

The app asks for no system permissions. It does not use the camera, the photo library, contacts, location, your health data, or notifications, and iOS will never prompt you to allow any of them, because the app never asks.

Deleting the app deletes all of it. There is no copy anywhere else to delete, and nothing to close down or request.

How data leaves, when you send it

Export writes your data to a JSON or CSV file and hands it to the standard iOS share sheet. You choose where it goes — AirDrop, Files, Mail, or anywhere else you have installed. The app never picks a destination and never sends anything on its own. Import is the same in reverse: it reads a file you pick.

Once a file is somewhere else, whatever is there governs it. If you mail an export to yourself, it is in your mail provider's hands; that is outside this app and outside this policy.

Device backups

The store file sits in the app's Application Support directory and is not marked to be excluded from backups. So if you have iPhone backups turned on, to iCloud or to a computer, your Boring Tracker data is included in them, exactly as other apps' data is. That is iOS doing what you have asked it to do, under Apple's terms; the app takes no part in it, and cannot read anything back from a backup other than by iOS restoring your device.

Children

The app collects nothing from anyone, so it collects nothing from children. It has no accounts, no ads, no messaging and no content served from anywhere.
